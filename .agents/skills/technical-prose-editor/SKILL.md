# Technical Prose Editor

Improve explanatory writing without applying mechanical style bans.

## Editing Pass

1. Preserve the author's technical claims, terminology, language, and intended difficulty unless a correction is required and verified.
2. Remove sentences that add no information, repeated explanations, empty transitions, canned introductions, fake emphasis, and unsupported superlatives.
3. Replace vague claims with concrete mechanisms, conditions, actors, and consequences.
4. Prefer active voice when the actor matters. Keep passive voice when the process or result is the subject and naming the actor would add noise.
5. Keep adverbs that carry technical meaning; remove only those that weaken or redundantly intensify a claim.
6. Vary sentence and paragraph structure naturally. Do not force artificial rhythm or fragments.
7. Preserve pedagogical scaffolding that bridges a real prerequisite or likely misconception. Compress hand-holding that merely restates the same idea.
8. Check analogies against the mechanism they explain. Remove or qualify analogies that create a false model.
9. Keep examples close to the claims they support. Remove examples that introduce more framework or syntax than the concept requires.
10. Read the revised section as a whole and repair transitions, references, and terminology made inconsistent by local edits.

## Common Defects

- announcements of what the text is about to say;
- repeated "in other words" restatements;
- generic claims such as "this is powerful" or "this is important";
- formulaic binary contrasts that distort nuance;
- three-part lists created only for rhetorical symmetry;
- anthropomorphic claims about code, systems, or abstractions;
- motivational filler unrelated to the learner's goal;
- summaries that repeat a short section verbatim.

## Boundaries

- Do not ban punctuation, passive voice, adverbs, questions, or any other grammatical construction categorically.
- Do not make prose telegraphic merely to shorten it.
- Do not change code behavior during a prose edit.
- Do not invent citations or technical corrections.
- Follow the repository language rules and preserve valid Obsidian syntax.
